About

I am an economist with expertise in impact evaluations of public policies and innovative interventions advancing women’s economic empowerment. My work focuses on how both formal norms, embedded in laws and policies, and social norms, embedded in behaviours and institutions, shape women’s economic opportunities — and how they can be transformed to unlock women’s human capital, productivity, and economic agency.

Since 2023, I have been working at the World Bank, within the Africa Gender Innovation Lab, where I evaluate field interventions and regulatory reforms affecting women’s access to assets, labour markets, finance, and services. Another core part of my research examines how technologically enabled interventions can be leveraged to relax constraints, support norm change, and scale impact.

I am currently leading a new joint research agenda between Women, Business and the Law and the Africa Gender Innovation Lab, bridging legal analysis and impact evaluation to strengthen the evidence base on policy design, implementation, and enforcement.

I received my PhD in Economics from the University of Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne and a Master’s degree in Law from the École Normale Supérieure.
